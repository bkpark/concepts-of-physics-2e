Sources and Adaptation History

Original textbook and earlier adaptations

OpenStax / Rice University. The principal source is College Physics, by OpenStax and its contributors, copyright Rice University. The source modules used for this collection were released under CC BY 4.0. Individual module titles, identifiers, versions, and attribution links are recorded in the chapter and appendix credits.

Bobby Bailey. Concepts of Physics adapted selected OpenStax modules for a one-semester course with reduced mathematical prerequisites. Its modifications included topic selection, changes to explanations, examples, exercises, figures, and notation, and removal of material outside the course’s scope.

James Rittenbach. Linear-momentum material was incorporated from his derived adaptation of Concepts of Physics.

Andrew Park, College of Alameda. Adapted and revised Introduction to Physics for a general-education conceptual physics course, restoring material including momentum, rotation, and special relativity and preparing the revisions in Version 1.1. The recovered OpenStax CNX collection is col25183, version 12.1. Its historical prefaces and source attribution records are preserved in the project.

Version 1.1 revisions and additional sources

This edition revises explanations and conceptual treatments, updates scientific data and SI definitions, curates chapter-end exercises, repairs mathematical notation and cross-references, and revises illustrations and page layout. The preface describes the editorial process and use of AI-assisted tools. The source repository records the revisions and their provenance.

OpenStax update comparisons use a preserved CC BY 4.0 source snapshot dated February 4, 2026. The repository identifies the exact version and its license evidence. This attribution applies to the source versions used here.

The Carnot-principle passage also draws on University Physics Volume 2, Section 4.5, “The Carnot Cycle,” by Samuel J. Ling, Jeff Sanny, and William Moebs, OpenStax / Rice University (2016), under CC BY 4.0. The principle is reproduced, the following equivalence sentence is adapted, and its placement is changed. The chapter credit provides the source link.

Scientific data sources and evaluation conventions are identified with their tables and passages, including the reference tables in Appendices A–C. Figure-specific credits remain with the figures.
