Maintained text

This same logic explains the behavior of gyroscopes. [reference] shows the two forces acting on a spinning gyroscope. The torque produced is perpendicular to the angular momentum, thus the direction of the angular momentum is changed but not its magnitude, as a result of the change in angular momentum produced by the torque. This type of motion is called a precession, and we say that the gyroscope precesses around a vertical axis, since the torque is always horizontal and perpendicular to L. If the gyroscope is not spinning, it acquires initial angular momentum in the direction of the torque (L=ΔL) and the change of angular momentum due to torque continues to add in the same direction, causing the gyroscope to rotate around a horizontal axis, falling over just as we would expect.
