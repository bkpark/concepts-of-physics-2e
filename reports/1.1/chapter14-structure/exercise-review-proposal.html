Based on the September 27, 2026 Physics 10 export: Question Set 12 and its assessment, Lectures 28–30, and the Chapter 14 timed multiple-choice and essay assessments. The 10-question homework emphasizes radiation identification, ionization, shielding, nuclear forces, isotope counts, whole-number half-lives, imaging, fission/fusion and particle classification. The timed variants stay at that level. Lecture 28 explicitly says dose-unit detail and much of the long chapter are optional. Numbers stay fixed. Removed exercises leave gaps. The surviving questions keep their current numbers and IDs. “Revise” includes a few clearly marked replacement prompts at the existing number; those need your approval. Before implementation, check numbering overrides, shared directions, solutions and inbound links. This packet makes no source edits.

Optional extension means an exercise worth retaining for extra practice, not a new expectation that students master every late-chapter topic. You can filter these as a group below. No new exercise numbers are proposed.

Decisions worth reviewing first

  • Overall size: retain 63 of 195, including 18 optional extensions.
  • New conceptual coverage in existing slots: 12 (ionization), 18 (isotope counts), 90 (imaging methods), 114 (fusion barrier), 164 (interactions), 169 (solar neutrinos), 185 (Standard Model versus GUTs). Exercise 167 changes a flawed massless-particle premise into an optional neutron question.
  • Four representative decay equations: 33, 35, 37 and 39. Supply atomic numbers; remove the long series of mass-table energy calculations.
  • Half-life: 67–68 use forward and backward repeated halving, matching the numerical level in the export. No logarithms are required.
  • Weapons subsection: retain environmental transport versus decay in 152 and one optional mass–energy calculation in 153; remove the specialized weapon-effect and fuel calculations.
  • Recently corrected exercises: keep 184; remove 179 and 190 on level/redundancy grounds. Earlier scientific corrections do not oblige us to retain a question.

MyOpenMath cautions

The export guides level, not scientific wording. These are follow-up leads; no live course changes are proposed here.

  • Radiation essay: remove the blanket “perfectly safe even in large quantities” framing in any adaptation. Non-ionizing radiation can still have harmful effects.
  • Radiation-safety variants: do not turn the time/distance/shielding mnemonic into a claim that contamination PPE or medically appropriate non-ionizing alternatives can never reduce exposure.
  • Radioactivity variants: distinguish beta-minus from beta-plus; describe gamma shielding as attenuation, not a guarantee that every photon stops.
  • Particle classification: hadrons are composite, so “categories of elementary particles” is misleading for proton/pion examples; “omega” needs an explicit symbol to distinguish Ω baryons from ω mesons.
  • Fission/fusion variants: avoid presenting the iron boundary as an exact universal reaction rule. Use the binding-energy trend. Lecture fusion status is time-sensitive and is not copied into the proposed prompts.

Factual checks used: EPA radiation protection; FDA X-ray imaging; FDA ultrasound; FDA MRI risks; SNO flavor-transformation measurement. The proposal is not clinical or radiation-workplace guidance.
