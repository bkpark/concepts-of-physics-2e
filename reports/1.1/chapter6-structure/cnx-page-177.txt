Introduction to Rotational Motion and Angular Momentum
Why do tornadoes spin at all? And why do tornados spin so rapidly? The answer is that air masses that produce tornadoes are
themselves rotating, and when the radii of the air masses decrease, their rate of rotation increases. An ice skater increases her
spin in an exactly analogous manner as seen in Figure 7.2. The skater starts her rotation with outstretched limbs and increases
her spin by pulling them in toward her body. The same physics describes the exhilarating spin of a skater and the wrenching
force of a tornado.
Clearly, force, energy, and power are associated with rotational motion. These and other aspects of rotational motion are covered
in this chapter. We shall see that all important aspects of rotational motion either have already been defined for linear motion or
have exact analogs in linear motion. First, we look at angular acceleration—the rotational analog of linear acceleration.
