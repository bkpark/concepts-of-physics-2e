# Atomic Masses numerical audit

No textbook edits made. All 160 rows matched to AME2020 and NUBASE2020 ground-state records. All 106 abundance-bearing rows matched to CIAAW 2024. Values and uncertainties are retained in comparison.json; discrepancies are not automatically corrections.

Sources:
- AME2020: https://amdc.impcas.ac.cn/masstables/Ame2020/mass_1.mas20

- CIAAW 2024: https://www.ciaaw.org/isotopic-abundances.htm

## Principal findings

- Copper-63 mass: 62.939598 u versus 62.929597119 u: clear numerical error.
- Silicon-28 incorrectly has 2.62 h; it is stable. Silicon-31 has a blank half-life; its evaluated 157.16 min is about 2.619 h.
- Uranium-233: 1.59e3 y versus 159.19 ky (1.5919e5 y): exponent error.
- Carbon-11 half-life is missing; NUBASE2020 gives 20.3402 min.
- Tellurium-130: 2.5e21 y versus 7.91e20 y; decay is double beta, not ordinary beta.
- Xenon-136 and bismuth-209 have blank decay/half-life fields despite known radioactivity.
- Hassium-264: 0.08 ms versus 0.7 s in NUBASE2020.
- Heavy-isotope state assignments need explicit review: for example Rf-261 ground state is 2.1 s, whereas a metastable state is 74 s. Do not blindly replace state-dependent data.
- 134 of 160 masses differ from AME2020 when rounded to the displayed decimal places. Most are small last-digit updates, not gross errors; uncertainty and estimated-mass flags must govern precision.
- Abundances are representative or ranges, not universal constants. Compare with CIAAW intervals/uncertainties before calling small differences errors.
